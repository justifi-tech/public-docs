# PayPal and Venmo

:::info Coming Soon
PayPal and Venmo are coming soon to JustiFi checkouts. This page describes how they will work.
:::

## Overview

PayPal and Venmo are payment options of a checkout. Your customer selects the PayPal or Venmo button,
approves the payment in PayPal or Venmo, and the checkout completes. You create and track the checkout the same way
you do for any other payment method.

## Prerequisites and Integration

- To enable PayPal and Venmo, contact [customer_success@justifi.tech](mailto:customer_success@justifi.tech)
  to activate PayPal for your platform and sub accounts.
- PayPal and Venmo are offered through our checkout web components
([Modular Checkout](https://docs.justifi.tech/web-components/modular-checkout)
and [Unified Checkout](https://docs.justifi.tech/web-components/payment-facilitation/unified-fintech-checkout%E2%84%A2)) - [see the integration guide](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Checkout-via-Component).
- Both options require the [creation of a checkout via API](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Checkouts/operation/CreateCheckout).
- If you use Unified Checkout, the PayPal and Venmo buttons appear automatically once PayPal is enabled on the sub account.
- If you use Modular Checkout, include the [PayPal sub component](https://docs.justifi.tech/web-components/modular-checkout/sub-components/paypal). One component renders both the PayPal and the Venmo button.
- The Venmo button is shown only to customers in the United States, on checkouts in USD.
- Checkouts in `test` mode use PayPal's sandbox.

## How it works

1. Create a checkout and render the checkout web component.
2. Your customer selects the PayPal or Venmo button and approves the payment in PayPal or Venmo.
3. The web component completes the checkout. You do not call the Complete Checkout API.
4. The checkout becomes `completed`, and the attempt is recorded in its `completions` with a `payment_mode` of `paypal` or `venmo`.

Your customer can approve the payment in PayPal or Venmo and then close your checkout page before the checkout completes.
In that case JustiFi still collects the PayPal or Venmo payment and completes the checkout, as long as the checkout is not expired and its amount has not changed.
Subscribe to the [checkout events](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Events/operation/checkoutCompletionEvent)
so you learn the outcome even when your customer has left your page.

## What is different from a card or ACH checkout

| | Card or ACH | PayPal or Venmo |
|---|---|---|
| Payment record | A payment (`py_...`) is created | **No JustiFi payment is created** |
| `successful_payment_id` on the checkout | The payment id | `null` |
| `payment_id` on the completion | The payment id | `null` |
| Events | Checkout events and payment events | Checkout events only |
| Refunds | Checkout or payment refund APIs | [Refund a Checkout API](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Checkouts/operation/RefundCheckout) only |
| Checkout fee parameters | Applied | Not applied |
| Statement descriptor | Shown as sent | PayPal adds a prefix such as `PAYPAL *` and shortens it to 22 characters |
| Payouts | Included in JustiFi payouts | Paid out by PayPal, outside of JustiFi payouts |

### No payment record

Because no JustiFi payment is created, the checkout is your record of the transaction:

- Store the checkout id. There is no payment id to pass to the [Payments API](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Payments).
- Subscribe to `checkout.completed`, `checkout.completion.succeeded` and `checkout.completion.failed`.
  No [payment events](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Events/operation/paymentEvent) are sent.

### The checkout completion

A successful PayPal completion looks like this:

```json
{
  "id": "chc_xyz",
  "checkout_id": "cho_xyz",
  "payment_mode": "paypal",
  "status": "succeeded",
  "payment_status": "succeeded",
  "payment_token": "ppo_xyz",
  "payment_id": null,
  "payment_method_id": null,
  "payment_response": {
    "id": "0UR96687T5480713P",
    "status": "COMPLETED",
    "status_reason": null,
    "amount": { "cents": 1000, "currency_iso": "USD" },
    "paypal_fee": null,
    "platform_fee": null,
    "net_amount": { "cents": 1000, "currency_iso": "USD" },
    "create_time": "2026-09-28T15:04:05.000Z"
  }
}
```

`payment_token` identifies the PayPal order your customer approved. `payment_response` holds the details PayPal returned
for the transaction. It is not a JustiFi payment object, so do not parse it as one.

## Pending completions

PayPal does not always confirm a payment right away. Until it does:

- The completion has a `status` and `payment_status` of `pending`.
- The checkout stays `attempted`.
- Another PayPal or Venmo attempt on the same checkout is refused with the error code `checkout_completion_pending`.

Wait for the events before you fulfill the order:

| PayPal's decision | Completion | Checkout | Events sent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | `succeeded` | `completed` | `checkout.completed`, `checkout.completion.succeeded` |
| Denied | `failed` | `attempted` | `checkout.completion.failed` |

## Updating a checkout

You can [update a checkout](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Checkouts/operation/UpdateCheckout) after your customer
has started a PayPal or Venmo payment. The new amount, description or statement descriptor also applies to that payment.
If PayPal does not accept the change, the request fails with a `422` and the error code `paypal_order_update_failed`,
and the checkout keeps its previous values.

## Refunds

Refund a PayPal or Venmo checkout with the [Refund a Checkout API](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Checkouts/operation/RefundCheckout).
The refund is issued through PayPal.

- You can refund the full amount or a portion. Multiple partial refunds are supported until the full amount has been refunded.
- A refund larger than the amount left to refund fails with a `422` and the error code `invalid_refund_amount`.
- The `fees` parameter is not applied.
- No refund (`re_...`) is created. The checkout refund (`chr_...`) is your record of the refund.

## Fees

The `application_fees` and `payment.fees` parameters of a checkout are not applied to PayPal and Venmo payments.

## Payouts

PayPal and Venmo payments are not included in JustiFi payouts. PayPal pays them out separately:

- Once a day, PayPal totals the previous day's PayPal and Venmo payments, minus refunds and chargebacks, and sends the total by ACH
  to the bank account provided during onboarding.
- Funds usually arrive 1 to 3 business days after PayPal sends them.

## Disputes

PayPal handles disputes and chargebacks on PayPal and Venmo payments. No JustiFi dispute is created.
The disputed amount and any chargeback fee are deducted from the merchant's next PayPal payout.

## Finding PayPal and Venmo checkouts

Use the `payment_mode` filter of the [List Checkouts API](https://docs.justifi.tech/api-spec#tag/Checkouts/operation/ListCheckouts)
with `paypal` or `venmo`.
